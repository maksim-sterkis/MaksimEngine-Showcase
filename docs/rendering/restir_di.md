# ReSTIR DI: Direct Lighting and Shadows

The sandbox implements initial candidate generation, temporal reuse, spatial reuse and final shading (**Phase 5 completed in `v0.3.0`, 2026-11-14**). This is **direct illumination only**: emitted light travels directly to the visible surface, with shadow-ray visibility; there are no secondary lighting bounces, indirect color bleeding or GI. The boulder retains its directional-sun renderer and remains the startup scene. In **Phase 5.1 (in progress)**, optional [DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction](dlss_integration.md#experimental-dlss-45-ray-reconstruction) (4-estimate decorrelated final shading), native Vulkan [NVIDIA NRD](nrd_integration.md) (`RELAX` with 4-estimate fresh RIS shading + emitter hit-distance, `REBLUR`, `SIGMA`), the two-room sandbox with `0.53°` directional sunlight, the measured Cornell Box, and output-resolution emitter presentation (`present_emitters.comp.spv`) are implemented. Intel New Sponza (2022) and extended validation continue in Phase 5.1, while custom SVGF stays deferred. [Cornell Box audit](../5.1/cornell-box.md)

The [2026-10-30 audit](../archive/phase-5/restir_di_audit.md) reviews initial estimator/history and performance findings. The [2026-11-01 follow-up](../archive/phase-5/phase5_history_followup.md) restores final-spatial history after independent CPU/GPU trajectory checks. ## User controls

The subsequent [rough-surface importance-target experiment](../archive/phase-5/phase5_target_proxy.md) passed correctness and quality gates but failed to establish a reliable performance win against the original shader modules. It was removed from production. Material-level audits and opt-in validation of the optimized Release executable remain; ordinary ranking still uses full-contribution luminance.

Select Lighting Sandbox in the session Esc scene selector. F5's Lighting Sandbox section offers **Conventional MC** and **ReSTIR DI**, with ReSTIR as the sandbox default. The current default uses four fresh candidates, a twenty-frame-equivalent history limit, one bounded spatial proposal and a sixteen-internal-pixel radius with **Basic** source correction. Disoccluded pixels try up to eight proposals. F5 retains ray-traced reference correction. Controls are session-only. See the [Nsight follow-up](../archive/phase-5/phase5_nsight_followup.md) for current measurements and verification limits.

Initial-only and temporal-only are explicitly incomplete **diagnostic ablations**, rather than separate main-menu claims of completed ReSTIR. Views show selected lights, effective sample counts, normalized weights, visibility-ray counts and reuse acceptance. Diagnostic views bypass reconstruction and restore the requested mode afterward. F5 reports effective fallback, allocation failures, allocated bytes and pass timings. Per-pixel rejection/ray counters are now separately opt-in; opening F4/F5 alone requests timestamps without enabling their global atomics. Allocation failure selects conventional sampling with an explicit reason and retry button; it does not silently claim active ReSTIR.

The existing unjittered Native 4096-spp reference always uses conventional sampling. It remains independent of ReSTIR and SDK history. Ordinary Native output has no radiance-history blending; averages saved by tests are validation artifacts, not a production denoiser.

## Sampling and correction

The estimator is an engine implementation of reservoir importance resampling, following the concepts in the [ReSTIR paper](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/bitterli2020spatiotemporal/) and [RTXDI integration/bias guidance](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXDI/blob/main/Doc/NoiseAndBias.md). No RTXDI SDK/runtime or third-party shader code is imported.

Light samples use stable triangle identity and barycentric coordinates. Sampling operates in the unit triangle's parameter measure: the uniform proposal density is `2 / triangleCount`, and the contribution includes the world-area Jacobian `2 * area`. This accounts for current and previous emitter area, including nonuniform scaling.

Emitter templates cache immutable material emission and the global emissive texture slot after asset remapping. The 112-byte source record keeps homogeneous vertex coordinates and uses `normalArea.xyz` for cached emission. GPU preparation preserves the original vertex transform calculation, computes world normals/area and moves emission into the world vertex W lanes. `uv2.z` holds the numeric texture slot. ReSTIR can therefore evaluate light emission without a dependent material-table lookup, while conventional reference shading still reads the original material table. Runtime material editing would need to refresh this cache; the current loader treats materials as immutable.

Fresh selection uses the luminance of the unoccluded direct RGB integrand as its target. Every proposal counts, including zero-target proposals. Reuse streams donor weights `M * W * destinationTarget`, then computes source-aware normalization from the selected sample's target/support and visibility at every contributing source. Each source uses its own surface, emission scale, light geometry and acceleration structure. Zero-support sources retain their proposal counts; their support term is zero. Visibility-off performs no correction or final shadow queries.

Ray-traced normalization evaluates current/previous source visibility and traces spatial source correction. Basic normalization uses unoccluded source targets, avoiding those correction segments and assuming visible source support. Both trace fresh final visibility and add visible emission once. Ray-traced mode has correction rays in addition to its final segment; Basic normally has only its final shadow segment. Samples are not discarded merely because final visibility is occluded. No lighting-value clamp conceals variance or invalid weights; numeric failures are counted and displayed.

## Data flow and history

The shared surface/material resolve caches a 48-byte surface. World position and depth use FP32, and instance, entity, surface, raster LOD and material IDs are exact. Geometric and shading normals use octahedral encoding with two signed 14-bit components each. Albedo uses FP16, and roughness and metallic use 12 bits each. Initial sampling and both reuse passes evaluate the decoded surface. Previous position and depth come from the existing transforms, with the CPU static-pose flag avoiding the transform round trip for unchanged models. The 32-byte packed reservoir preserves full-width index, count and age and FP32 sample coordinates, target and weight. Reserved flag bits hold integer diagnostics, the light table provides identity, and raw streaming sums stay private to the shader.

Three reservoir arrays are used:

1. Basic material/initial resolve normally feeds private initial samples directly through shared temporal reuse into scratch. The separate/reference path still writes the current initial array and reads it in a temporal kernel.
2. Scalar spatial reuse handles accepted temporal history; a general kernel handles disocclusion. They read temporal scratch and write the consumed initial array. Final shading is fused into the Basic spatial kernels; reference/ablation paths retain a separate resolve. Every final visibility segment is fresh.
3. After successful submission, the final spatial output remains in the current history slot by default. The optional temporal-only comparison instead rotates temporal scratch into that slot. GPU synchronization protects accesses; neither policy requires a full-resolution copy.

**Final spatial reservoirs become next-frame history by default.** The older brightening failure did not reproduce in the current Basic full GPU suite. Strict CPU tests and independently seeded GPU trajectories now check whole-image, regional and early/late agreement without widening tolerances. This supports restoration on the tested fixtures, not a universal bias proof or a mathematical identification of the historical failure. F5 retains a temporal-only history checkbox for comparison. Empty/background records are overwritten; inactive retained allocations are invalidated rather than reused.

Both surface snapshots, both temporal arrays, scratch, light snapshots, maps and counters are owned outside SDK history. Resources are allocated lazily for ReSTIR, cached across scene switches, resized only after retiring users, and released at shutdown. A 320-byte context supplies buffer addresses through set 3 binding 15, keeping descriptor pressure bounded. Existing buffer-device-address support is reused. Legacy initial-only test storage at binding 14 remains separate.

Current pixel resources use `2 * 48 + 3 * 32 = 192` bytes per internal pixel, plus light, map, context and counter allocations. The same allocation configuration totals 177,070,848 bytes at 1280x720 and 951,214,848 bytes at 3440x1440. F5 reports the actual ReSTIR DI allocation. Geometry, HDR, guide, reference and SDK targets are additional.

## Motion, lights and synchronization

Current/previous light mapping compares the full stable key: entity, model generation/handle, source surface, triangle and global material. Dense light/instance reordering preserves history; incompatible light-set or geometry/material changes reset it. Source maps and snapshots advance only with successful submissions. Moving lights and intensity/area changes reweight against their actual previous data instead of forcing a reset every frame.

Physical pose history survives lighting and SDK-history resets. Reprojection uses the visible instance's current/previous transforms, previous camera and jittered projection. It requires matching entity/surface/LOD/material, transformed geometric and shading normals with dot product at least 0.9, and previous depth within `max(0.01 m, 1% of expected previous depth)`. Spatial sources additionally pass a plane-distance gate and roughness agreement. The remaining first-object previous-depth calculation is corrected to use the owning instance.

Two TLAS snapshots retain actual previous geometry, including all opaque offscreen occluders and their selected immutable LOD BLAS. Current/previous AS descriptors are frame-owned at set 2 bindings 12/13, replacing global mutable-AS lookup in lighting. Source-frame visibility never substitutes the current TLAS for the previous one.

Compute/transfer barriers separate generation, history copy, spatial reuse and shading. Frame/image fences protect uploads and descriptor updates. Swapchain recreation preserves the submission-slot/fence phase and invalidates affected histories, including odd-size window resize/restore. Visibility remains 64 bits, draw push constants 256 bytes, temporal `FrameGpu` 704 bytes and the scene header 64 bytes. Existing MLOD assets and checked-in boulder files are unchanged.

## Verification and limits

Development uses MinGW Makefiles Debug, focused tests enabled, DLSS enabled and pinned Streamline 2.14.16. The full GPU suite is opt-in with `VKE_RESTIR_FULL_SMOKE=1`, `VKE_FIXED_PRESENT_PROBE=1`, `VK_LAYER_VALIDATE_SYNC=1` and `VKE_RECON_MODE=0`. It uses one decorated window; resize cases change only that window, not monitor/display modes or persisted settings.

The CPU suite adds independent finite-integral checks for source-aware normalization, disjoint visibility, newly introduced light support, unequal/history-capped counts, model/material identity changes, light-index permutation and overflow. Existing temporal, reconstruction, Hi-Z, compiler, texture-role and asset tests remain enabled.

The GPU suite compares Initial8, Temporal8, Full8 and MC8 against a 4096-spp conventional reference for three/64 emitters with shadows on/off. Each method warms for 64 frames and measures 512 frames at 320x180 internal Native rendering. Receiver-only RGB/error measurements exclude emissive surfaces and background. Standard errors use 64-frame blocks to account for temporal correlation. Brightness acceptance is six block standard errors plus 1% of the reference and a 0.0001 numeric margin. Many-light noise improvement over initial-only must exceed three block standard errors.

Further cases check actual reuse, finite weights, cached normals, instance-index bits, shadow-ray counts, light-off/on, reordered instances/lights, moving objects/lights, per-instance motion, light birth/death, cuts, LOD changes, emitter-area changes, Hi-Z visibility parity, Native/DLAA/Quality, diagnostic bypass/restoration, internal resolution changes and odd output resize/restore. Native and DLAA exercise 1280x720 internal resources. GPU image/buffer readbacks and device-idle waits in these assertions are test-only; ordinary frames use asynchronous telemetry only when diagnostics/timing are requested.

On NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 / driver 621.01, all **eleven CPU/offline suites** pass. The full DI suite, original initial-RIS suite and sandbox/boulder regression suite all pass with synchronization validation and no Vulkan validation/hazard messages. Twelve affected SPIR-V modules pass offline Vulkan 1.3/scalar-layout validation. Existing boulder assets are unchanged; `git diff --check` passes. The built engine is `build/VK_game_engine.exe`.

## 3440x1440 performance investigation

Baseline 320x180 reference checks shaded **86 times fewer pixels** than native 3440x1440 DLAA; evaluating full ReSTIR lighting at full output resolution significantly increases shadow-ray and reservoir-reuse cost (~20–30 FPS in early Debug runs).

Finite, focused 3440x1440 Mailbox probes on the same RTX 3090 identify spatial reuse as the largest GPU cost. The initial default external view measures 52.9 FPS / 17.54 ms GPU, including 10.03 ms spatial reuse and 3.39 ms temporal reuse. Disabling the counters gives 52.3 FPS; they do not explain the large slowdown. A heavier interior view with 64 emitters remains expensive after the first optimizations: about 42 FPS / 22.17 ms GPU, with 13.51 ms spatial reuse and only 1.35 ms DLAA. Exact frame rates depend strongly on view coverage and light count; full-DI allocations alone are 1,902,306,048 bytes at this resolution.

The first optimizations preserve the estimator, candidate count, accepted-neighbor policy, support/visibility correction and final shadow rays:

- Spatial reuse retains nine donor offsets instead of arrays of nine complete surfaces and reservoirs, reducing source-level temporary array storage per invocation from roughly 1.5 KiB to 36 bytes. The correction loop reloads the required immutable donor fields; hardware spill counts were not measured directly.
- Temporal reprojection checks the nearest texel first and prunes candidates whose distance cannot improve the accepted match, preserving the original tie order and all rejection gates.
- Reuse/shading dispatches use 8x8 workgroups instead of 16x16, with matching host dispatch dimensions. Deferred material/initial resolve remains 16x16.
- Counter collection is independent of timestamps. The full correctness suite explicitly enables it; interactive F4/F5 defaults to timestamps alone.

The improved default external view, **with both F4 and F5 actually drawn**, measures 73.3 FPS / 12.54 ms GPU with counters, or 74.8 FPS / 12.48 ms without. Spatial reuse falls to 6.00 ms and temporal reuse to 2.37 ms. These are view-specific Debug observations rather than across-the-board throughput guarantees; the interior remains around 40–45 FPS before Release/Phase 5 optimizations. Shader `-O` and a smaller spatial retry budget did not materially improve the measured cost and were not retained.

`VKE_RESTIR_PROFILE=1`, `VKE_FIXED_PRESENT_PROBE=1`, `VKE_PROBE_BORDERLESS=1`, `VKE_PROBE_FOCUS=1`, `VKE_RECON_MODE=1`, `VKE_TEST_VSYNC=0` run the finite probe. Optional `VKE_PROFILE_MANY=1` and `VKE_PROFILE_INTERIOR=1` select the heavier view. Seven cases each warm 60 frames and measure 180: full with counters, full without, temporal-only, initial-only, MC8, full without shadows, then full again. Interior probes add an eighth moving-camera case. Each logs median asynchronous pass timings, loop rate, focus, mode, resolution and storage; it checks sufficient samples and active ReSTIR. Counter cases also log receiver count, rejection/acceptance and actual temporal/spatial/final shadow segments. No pixel readbacks or device-idle waits occur in the measurement loop. Logs are `build/restir-ultrawide-before.log`, `restir-ultrawide-compact.log`, `restir-ultrawide-workgroup.log`, `restir-ultrawide-final.log`, `restir-ultrawide-interior.log`, `restir-ultrawide-interior-final.log` and `restir-ultrawide-three-final.log`.

The final 64-emitter moving-camera case measures 50.6 FPS, with 3.82 million receiver pixels, 3.81 million temporal acceptances and only 18,067 rejections in its last completed counter sample. It traces about **27.85 million shadow segments per frame** (6.92 million temporal correction, 17.44 million spatial correction, 3.48 million final). Ordinary camera motion does not show a global-history-reset problem in this probe. Repeated target evaluation, buffer traffic and correction work dominate the reuse passes; disabling shadows alone leaves the pass expensive. This implementation has substantially more work than a single shadow segment per receiver.

The final three-emitter interior probe measures 50.9 FPS stationary and 51.9 FPS with the prescribed camera motion. Its moving case has 3.81 million receivers and about 24.95 million shadow segments per frame. All three-emitter cases retain focus for 180/180 samples. In `build/restir-ultrawide-interior-final.log`, however, the 64-emitter stage 1 records 118/180 focused samples and stage 2 records 71/180; exclude these two cases from controlled paired comparisons and rerun them. Its stages 0 and 7 are fully focused. These Debug observations locate the reuse cost prior to Phase 5 Release optimizations; further work targeted reuse evaluation, surface packing, and buffer access rather than relying solely on denoisers.

After the retained changes, all eleven CPU/offline suites and four affected SPIR-V validations pass again. `build/restir-full-performance-regression.log` passes the entire 512-frame/method reference matrix, history/light/motion/area/LOD/Hi-Z cases and odd-size resize/restore without Vulkan validation messages. Recorded receiver means and MSEs match the earlier comparison at the reported precision. Counters are explicitly enabled by that suite. Assets remain unchanged and `git diff --check` passes.
